Overview

Learning from experience, weights fixed

The harness turns a general-purpose model into an operating agent. It can be revised without access to weights, and each revision is a named state transition — but an update is not an improvement. Whether an accepted change helped has to be shown, not assumed.

Definition

What counts as harness self‑evolution

A harness \(H\) is the software and configuration around a model \(M\) that governs its execution in an environment \(E\). The evaluation procedure \(J\) sits outside \(H\), so a change in \(J\) is a change in the measurement, not a harness update.

Rollout

Run the agent and score its trajectory.

\[\tau \sim \operatorname{Run}(M, H, E)\]
\[y = J(\tau)\]

Update round \(k\)

Collect a batch of trajectories, then revise the harness.

\[\tau_{k,i} \sim \operatorname{Run}(M, H_k, E_{k,i})\]
\[\mathcal{B}_k = \{\tau_{k,i}\}_{i=1}^{b}\]
\[H_{k+1} = \operatorname{Update}(H_k, \mathcal{B}_k)\]

\(\tau\): trajectory · \(y\): score · \(b\): batch size

\(\operatorname{Update}\) includes diagnosis, proposal, evaluation, and retention. Rejection leaves \(H_{k+1}=H_k\). The model \(M\) stays fixed.

The operational update loop

  1. 01
    ExecuteRun the current harness.
  2. 02
    FeedbackCollect outcomes and execution traces.
  3. 03
    ProposeForm a candidate revision.
  4. 04
    AdmitEvaluate and accept or reject the change.
  5. 05
    PersistSave the accepted state for later runs.

↳ Later runs load the accepted state and repeat the loop.

Persistence ≠ activation ≠ improvement

Operational feedback & continuity

The agent's own execution feedback drives an algorithmic update, and the mechanism stays in place so later runs can trigger further updates.

Persistence across runs

An accepted state is persisted and actually loaded by a later run. Whether it then operated is the activation claim, not a condition of scope.

Fixed base model

The analysis fixes model weights to isolate the harness effect. Co-evolving systems are read for their harness arm; changing models requires an attribution control.

Retention Regressions accumulate

\[F_{s,k} \le \sum_{u=s+1}^{k}\varepsilon_{s,u}\]

Forgetting telescopes over rounds, so even small tolerated regressions add up.

Admission Confidence-adjusted gate

\[\widehat\Delta_k(H') + \sqrt{\frac{2\ln(KT/\delta)}{m}} < 0\]

For at most K candidates in each of T rounds, use m fresh independent tasks and bounded paired losses. Under a fixed model and evaluator, the gate covers the evaluated candidate on that round’s task mixture. Repeated rollouts reduce noise without adding independent tasks.

Evaluation

Four claims behind a task score

An update can be stored and never used, used without being the cause, or be the cause only on the tasks that produced it — and the score looks the same in all three cases. Three claims must hold in turn; selection conditions every number they are read off.

Condition

Selection

Was the number measured, not picked?

No outcome on the reporting tasks fed the proposal, ranking, admission, or choice of round and configuration.

Step 1

Activation

Did the update operate in the scored runs?

Bind direct records to the scored runs: available, reached, or carried. Record whether coverage is one case, a sample, or all runs.

Step 2

Attribution

Did the difference come from the update?

Arms differing only in the update, controlled inference compute, and a margin beyond measured execution variation.

Step 3

Improvement

Does the difference hold elsewhere?

Located by distance from the tasks that formed the update, baseline, and round.

Reported evidence

Evidence for the reported gains

The manuscript examines the evidence for 96 methods, using one headline comparison per method. Two have no empirical evaluation, leaving 94 scored reports. Verdicts describe the evidence within its recorded coverage; they do not rank method quality.

Verdicts per claim

Each bar covers 94 scored reports. Criteria differ across claims; full Activation can be based on one confirmed case, with coverage recorded for each method.

Full evidencePartialNot supported

Claims at full evidence, per report

Partial evidence is not counted here.

  • The claims are rarely established together. The most common profile is selection and improvement at full evidence, with activation and attribution partial.
  • Claims about the scored runs are the least supported. Most reports show an artifact was loaded, or ran during search, rather than that it operated in the scored runs; most compare scores without measured variation or a controlled envelope.
  • Improvement is measured at one setting. A held-out or transfer result at the accepting round does not establish retention through later updates. The baseline and round are recorded separately from task distance.

Applications & outlook

What changes across deployment settings

Personal workspaces, enterprise processes, science, embodied interaction, and public services differ in how they share state and verify revisions. Open questions concern search efficiency, retention, dynamic evaluation, and oversight.
